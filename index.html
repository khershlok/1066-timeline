1066: before, during and after Hastings

The big dots are the events you must know. "Not certain" means the sources disagree.

Before the battle

Three men want the crown: Harold, William and Harald Hardrada.

  1. 1064Harold promises to help William become king Not certainHe swears this in Normandy. Breaking it later gives William an excuse to invade.
  2. 5 Jan 1066King Edward the Confessor diesHe has no children, so there is no obvious heir.
  3. 6 Jan 1066Harold is crowned kingWilliam is furious and starts planning an invasion.
  4. 8 Sept 1066Harold sends his soldiers homeThey had guarded the south coast all summer, but food ran out.
  5. 20 Sept 1066Norwegians win the Battle of FulfordHarald Hardrada of Norway invades the north and beats Earls Edwin and Morcar.
  6. 25 Sept 1066Harold wins the Battle of Stamford BridgeHe rushes north and kills Hardrada, but his army is now tired.
  7. 28 Sept 1066William lands at PevenseyNobody is guarding the coast, so he lands without a fight.
  8. Early Oct 1066Harold marches back southAbout 250 miles, with a tired army that isn’t complete.

The battle: 14 October 1066

It lasts all day, on a hill near Hastings.

  1. MorningThe English form a shield wall on top of a hillThe Normans have to attack uphill.
  2. MorningNorman attacks failArrows hit shields, and soldiers and horsemen can’t break the wall.
  3. Late morningSome Normans panic and runA rumour says William is dead. He lifts his helmet to show he is alive, and his men fight on.
  4. AfternoonNormans pretend to retreat Not certainEnglish soldiers chase them downhill and are killed. The shield wall gets weaker.
  5. EveningHarold is killed Not certainMaybe by an arrow in the eye, maybe by knights. The sources disagree.
  6. DuskThe English run awayWilliam has won the battle.

After the battle

William wins the crown, then has to keep it.

  1. Oct 1066The English choose Edgar Ætheling as kingHe is a teenager and is never crowned.
  2. Oct–Nov 1066William takes Dover, Canterbury and WinchesterHe punishes towns that resist and spares those that surrender.
  3. Dec 1066English leaders surrender at BerkhamstedWilliam has burned the land around London, so they give up.
  4. 25 Dec 1066William is crowned kingIn Westminster Abbey, on Christmas Day.
  5. 1067 onwardsNormans build castles all over EnglandIncluding the Tower of London. Castles help a few Normans control many English.
  6. 1068–71The English rebel several timesIncluding Edwin and Morcar, and Hereward the Wake. All the rebellions fail.
  7. 1069–70The Harrying of the NorthWilliam destroys farms and villages in Yorkshire. Thousands starve.
  8. 1086The Domesday BookA survey of who owns what. Almost all the land now belongs to Normans.

Remember

Why William won: Harold’s army was tired from Stamford Bridge, the coast was unguarded when William landed, and William was a clever leader.

How William kept control: castles, crushing rebellions and giving English land to Normans.
